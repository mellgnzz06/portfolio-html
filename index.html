Asthma and Air Quality in the Bronx

The Bronx has the highest asthma emergency room rates in New York City. I wanted to know whether dirty air explains it, so I compared air pollution, poverty and asthma ER visits for every community district in the city.

Main finding

Asthma ER visit rates track neighborhood poverty (r = +0.84), not measured PM2.5 air pollution (r = −0.01).

The question

Do NYC neighborhoods with more fine-particle air pollution (PM2.5) have higher asthma emergency department visit rates, and does that hold after accounting for neighborhood poverty? I compared all 59 community districts instead of only the Bronx's 12, because 12 is too few for reliable statistics.

How I did it

  1. Read the backgroundRead the NYC Community Air Survey report and Health Department data stories to learn how PM2.5 is measured and how asthma rates are reported.
  2. Collected five official datasetsDownloaded adult and child asthma ED visit rates, PM2.5, NO2 and poverty rates from the NYC Environment & Health Data Portal.
  3. Cleaned and merged the dataWrote a Python script with pandas that filters each file to 2023 community-district values and joins them into one table: 59 rows, no missing values.
  4. Described and charted itCompared borough averages, ranked districts, and drew scatter plots with Bronx districts highlighted.
  5. Ran the statisticsCalculated Pearson correlations, then multiple regressions with both PM2.5 and poverty so each one's effect could be seen separately.
  6. Checked the resultRe-ran the analysis without Manhattan, whose commercial core has high PM2.5 but few residents, and looked at which Bronx districts sit above or below what the model predicts.

  • Six of the city's ten highest adult rates are in the Bronx. Mott Haven and Melrose is highest, at 250 per 10,000.
  • PM2.5 varies little across the city (6.1 to 8.4 µg/m³) and is highest in Midtown and lower Manhattan, where asthma rates are low.
  • Outside Manhattan, PM2.5 does correlate with asthma (r = +0.32), but the link disappears once poverty is in the model.
  • Bronx rates are about what its poverty predicts. Williamsbridge and Morris Park sit well above prediction, which makes them good places to study next.

What it means

Comparing neighborhoods' yearly averages cannot separate the effect of air from the effect of poverty and what comes with it, such as older housing, less regular asthma care and more stress. This doesn't mean air pollution is harmless: studies of individuals and of daily pollution spikes show that PM2.5 does trigger asthma attacks.

Limitations: one year of data (2023), neighborhood averages rather than individuals, modeled PM2.5, and other factors like housing quality and smoking not measured.

CRISPR-Cas9 and Inherited Blood Disorders

An independent research paper on how CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing is being applied to treat sickle cell disease and beta-thalassemia, written to make the science clear for a general reader.

Focus

How a gene-editing tool moved from the lab to treatment for two inherited blood disorders.

Why this topic

Sickle cell disease and beta-thalassemia are inherited disorders of hemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Both are caused by changes in a single gene, which makes them strong candidates for gene editing. I wanted to understand how CRISPR-Cas9 works and how researchers are using it against these diseases.

How I did it

  1. Defined the scopeNarrowed a broad topic, gene editing, to one tool and two related diseases so the paper could go into real depth.
  2. Reviewed the scientific literatureFound and read research articles and reviews on CRISPR-Cas9 and its use in treating sickle cell disease and beta-thalassemia.
  3. Organized the evidenceGrouped what I read into the biology of each disease, how CRISPR-Cas9 works, and how it is being applied as a treatment.
  4. Translated it into clear writingWrote the paper so that complex molecular biology could be followed by a reader without a science background.
  5. Revised and submittedEdited the draft for accuracy and clarity, then submitted the finished research paper.

What I learned

  • How to search for, read and evaluate primary research and review articles.
  • How a single-gene change leads to disease, and how editing can target it.
  • How to explain technical science in plain language, a skill I use when talking with patients and customers.

Simple Health Tracker

A Python program that tracks daily habits such as water intake and sleep. It asks the user for each day's numbers, checks them, stores them, and displays the results.

Goal

Turn everyday health habits into data a person can actually see.

How I did it

  1. Planned what to trackChose simple daily habits that affect health, starting with water intake and hours of sleep.
  2. Took user inputWrote prompts that ask the user to enter each day's values.
  3. Checked the entries with conditionalsUsed if/else logic to respond to what the user entered.
  4. Stored and displayed the data with loopsUsed loops to collect entries over multiple days and print them back to the user.
  5. Tested and debuggedRan the program with different inputs and fixed errors, using the debugging habits I built in CSCI 111.

The core idea

A simplified sketch of the program's structure:

days = [] for day in range(7): water = float(input("Cups of water today: ")) sleep = float(input("Hours of sleep last night: ")) days.append((water, sleep)) for i, (water, sleep) in enumerate(days, start=1): print(f"Day {i}: {water} cups, {sleep} hours of sleep")

Next version

  • Save entries to a CSV file so data is kept between runs.
  • Draw weekly charts with matplotlib, the same tool I used in my asthma research.
